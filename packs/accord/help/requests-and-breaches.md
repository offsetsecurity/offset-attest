# Requests and breaches

Both have a legal deadline. Both screens show a countdown.

## Requests from individuals

Anyone can ask what data you hold about them, or ask you to correct, delete,
restrict or stop using it. They do not have to use special words or a form.

1. Log it in **Requests** the day it arrives. The **received** date starts the
   clock.
2. Check who they are, if you are not sure. Do not ask for more than you need.
3. Find all their data. The data map tells you where to look.
4. Answer within **one month**.
5. If it is complex, you may extend by two more months. Tell the person within
   the first month, with the reason. Set **Extended** to Yes and write the
   reason; the countdown moves.
6. Record what you did in **Outcome** and close it.

A request can be refused when it is clearly unfounded or excessive. Record the
reason. The person can complain to the regulator.

## Breaches

A personal data breach is any security problem that leads to personal data
being lost, destroyed, changed, shared or accessed by mistake or on purpose.
An email sent to the wrong person counts.

1. Log it in **Breaches** as soon as you know. The **became aware** time starts
   the 72-hour clock.
2. Stop it getting worse. Write what you did in **Containment**.
3. Assess the risk to the people affected.
4. **Tell the regulator?** Yes, unless it is unlikely to harm anyone. You have
   72 hours. If you are late, write why.
5. **Tell the people affected?** Yes, if it is high risk to them.
6. Write down the reasons for both decisions. GDPR asks for this on every
   breach, including the ones you do not report.
7. Close it when the work is done.

The breach log is one of the first things a regulator asks for.
