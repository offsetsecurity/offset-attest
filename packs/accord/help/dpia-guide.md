# Doing a DPIA

A DPIA (data protection impact assessment) is a structured look at a risky use
of personal data, done **before** you start. Article 35 makes it compulsory for
processing that is likely to be high risk to people.

## 1. Screening: do you need one?

Open **DPIAs → Add DPIA** and link it to the data map entry. Answer the nine
screening questions. They follow the signs European regulators use:

- scoring or profiling people
- automatic decisions with big effects
- watching or tracking people
- sensitive or very personal data
- large scale
- combining data sets
- vulnerable people, such as children, patients or staff
- new technology
- stopping people using a service or a right

**Two or more Yes answers:** you need a DPIA. **One:** think carefully and
write down why. **None:** set the status to **Not needed** and save. The
screening stays on file as your reason.

## 2. Describe it

What the processing is, why it is needed, and whether there is a less
intrusive way. How you keep data to the minimum. How people are told and can
use their rights.

## 3. Score the risks

For each risk to people (not to your organisation), pick how **likely** it is
and how **severe** it would be. Write what you will do about it, then the risk
that is **left over** after that.

The highest left-over risk becomes the DPIA's overall result.

## 4. Decide

- Ask your Data Protection Officer, if you have one, and record their advice.
- Record the decision: go ahead, go ahead with changes, or do not go ahead.
- If **high** risk is left and you still want to go ahead, you must consult
  the regulator first (Article 36). Set **Consult the regulator** to Yes, then
  Done when it is done.

## 5. Review

Set a review date. The owner is reminded when it comes. Review sooner if the
processing changes.
