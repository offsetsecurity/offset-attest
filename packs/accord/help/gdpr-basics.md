# GDPR in plain words

The General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) is the EU law on personal data.
It applies to any organisation that uses personal data about people in the EU,
wherever the organisation is.

## What counts as personal data

Anything that can identify a living person, on its own or with other data: a
name, an email address, a phone number, an ID number, a photo, a location, an
IP address.

Some data is **special category** and needs extra care: health, ethnicity,
religion, politics, union membership, genetics, biometrics, sex life and sexual
orientation. Criminal records have their own rules too.

## The seven principles (Article 5)

1. **Lawful, fair and open.** Have a lawful reason, and tell people.
2. **Purpose.** Use data only for the reason you collected it.
3. **Minimum.** Collect only what you need.
4. **Accurate.** Keep it right and up to date.
5. **Not too long.** Delete it when you no longer need it.
6. **Secure.** Protect it against loss and misuse.
7. **Accountable.** Be able to prove all of the above.

The last one is why this product exists. Most of GDPR is about being able to
show your records.

## The six lawful bases (Article 6)

| Basis | Typical use |
|---|---|
| Consent | Marketing emails, optional cookies |
| Contract | Delivering what a customer ordered, paying staff |
| Legal obligation | Tax records, health and safety records |
| Vital interests | A medical emergency |
| Public task | Public bodies doing their official job |
| Legitimate interests | Security, fraud prevention, some analytics |

## Rights people have

Access, correction, erasure, restriction, portability, objection, and
protection from purely automated decisions. You have **one month** to answer.

## Breaches

If personal data is lost, stolen, changed or exposed, tell the regulator within
**72 hours**, unless it is unlikely to harm anyone. If it is high risk to
people, tell them too. Log **every** breach, even the ones you do not report.
