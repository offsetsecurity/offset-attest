# Common questions

## Does Attest issue a SOC report?

No. Only a licensed CPA firm can examine your controls and issue a SOC 1,
SOC 2 or SOC 3 report. Attest is where you prepare: scope, criteria, evidence,
tests and the documents the firm asks for.

## Does this connect to my cloud accounts?

No. Every record is written by a person, and nothing leaves this server. The
guide **Collecting evidence from the cloud** says where to find the proof in
AWS, Azure, Microsoft 365 and Google Workspace.

## SOC 1, SOC 2 or SOC 3?

**SOC 2** is about the security of your service, and is what most customers
ask for. **SOC 1** is for services that affect your customers' financial
statements, such as payroll, billing or claims processing. **SOC 3** is a short,
public version of a SOC 2 that you can publish on your website. See **SOC
reports in plain words**.

## Type I or Type II?

A Type I tests that controls are designed properly at one date. A Type II tests
that they also worked throughout a period, usually 3 to 12 months. Customers
usually want a Type II. Many companies start with a Type I to get something in
hand sooner.

## Which categories should be in scope?

Security, always. Add Availability if you promise uptime. Add Confidentiality
if you handle confidential customer data under contract. Processing integrity
and Privacy are less common. Every category you add is more criteria to meet
and test.

## Why are there SOC 1 control objectives in the list?

Because a SOC 1 has no fixed criteria. The service organisation writes its own
control objectives, and these twelve are a starting set to edit. If you are
not doing a SOC 1, mark them out of scope in **Scope**.

## What does the auditor's opinion mean?

**Unmodified** is the clean opinion everyone wants. **Qualified** means some
criteria were not met. Exceptions can appear in a report with an unmodified
opinion. They are listed, and customers read them.

## Who gets reminder emails?

The owner email on each record: policies, vendors, training, internal audits
and SOC reports, as their dates come. Criteria owners are reminded of their due
dates.
