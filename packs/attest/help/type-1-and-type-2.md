# Type I and Type II

## The difference

A **Type I** report says your controls were suitably designed and in place on
one date. The auditor looks at each control once.

A **Type II** report says your controls were suitably designed and also
operated effectively throughout a period, usually between 3 and 12 months. The
auditor samples each control across the period: some quarterly access reviews,
some changes, some new starters and leavers.

Customers usually want a Type II, because it shows controls kept working, not
just that they exist.

## A common path

1. **Readiness** (one to three months). Scope, risk assessment, policies,
   controls in place. Get ready in Attest walks through this.
2. **Type I** (optional). An early report to give customers while the Type II
   period runs.
3. **Type II period** (three to six months for a first report). Every control
   runs and leaves evidence. Nothing is tested yet; it is all being recorded.
4. **Fieldwork** (two to six weeks after the period ends). The auditor samples
   the evidence, interviews owners and writes up any exceptions.
5. **Report issued**, usually within a few weeks of fieldwork.
6. **The next period** starts the day after the last one ended, and usually
   runs twelve months.

## What to record in Attest

In **SOC reports**, add one record per report:

- **Type I**: leave Period starts empty and put the "as of" date in Period ends.
- **Type II**: put both dates. Attest refuses a period that ends before it starts.
- When the report arrives, set the status to Report issued, the date, the
  opinion and any exceptions.
- Set **Next report due**. A reminder goes out ahead of it, so there is no gap
  between periods.

## Gaps and bridge letters

If customers ask about the time between the end of your last period and today,
your CPA firm can't cover it, but you can give them a **bridge letter** (also
called a gap letter). It is your own statement that nothing material has
changed. Keep it short and keep the gap short.
