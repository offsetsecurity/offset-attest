# Your first hour

Six things, in this order. None of them takes long.

## 1. Add the people who own controls

**Users → Add someone.** Add the people who run controls day to day:
engineering, IT, HR, security and finance. Give each person the smallest role
that lets them do their job.

| Role | Can do |
|---|---|
| Administrator | Everything, plus adding people and changing settings |
| Contributor | Read and change all records. Most people |
| Auditor | Read everything, including the audit trail. Change nothing. Give this to your CPA firm |
| Read only | Read everything. Change nothing |

## 2. Turn on email

**Settings → Outgoing email.** Put in your mail server and send yourself a test
message.

Without it nobody hears that an access review, a vendor review or a policy
review is due. A recurring control that is missed once shows up in a Type II
report as an exception.

## 3. Decide the scope

**Scope.** Security is always in a SOC 2. Keep Availability, Processing
integrity, Confidentiality or Privacy only if customers need them, and mark the
rest out of scope with a one-line reason. If you are not doing a SOC 1, rule
out the SOC 1 control objectives as well.

## 4. Record the report you are aiming for

**SOC reports → Add.** For example "SOC 2 Type II, 2027", with the categories
in scope. Fill in the CPA firm and the period once they are agreed.

## 5. Put your name and logo on reports

**Reports**: set the organisation name and upload your logo. The SOC 3
draft and the control matrices go to your auditor and later to customers.

## 6. Keep a backup somewhere else

**Backups → Download** the newest one, and keep that file off this server.

After that, open **Get ready**. It shows what to do next.
